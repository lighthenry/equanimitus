Who am I?
==============

This site serves as a collection of my attempts at investigating human nature. The goal is to devise a system, or a set of principles, for disciplined focus. I want to understand what will work for me personally, and in doing so relay my findings to this site. What this site will become I am not yet sure. But through a process of continuous improvement I am seeking to build a site that can be of practical use for each user - be it through the information provided, the community created, or an appreciation of the process followed.

I trained as an architect and have been involved in various construction projects of varying scales throughout my time doing that job. Dan Holy is not my real name, I like to think of myself as a pretty private person. My career thus far has largely centred around an ability to communicate often abstract ideas to people. The vision in your mind is never the vision in someone else's. Precise, explicit, and believable instruction was key, and I think it's in this need to effectively communicate that has taken me to where I am now. I am attempting to distill the thoughts in my head, the lessons I have learnt, and those I have not yet learnt, into words for the benefit of myself and others.

During my younger years, from about the age of 16 until 24, I struggled greatly with discipline. I think at high school perhaps this proved less of an issue. High school was a pretty competitive place to be for me. My friends were mostly getting good scores on tests, and they were aiming high. They had careers and univiersities in mind early on. I never really felt I knew what I wanted. The lack of aim at the time did not seem to matter though, because high school was about beating your friends. You looked up to people. You tried to do what they did. Falling behind wasn't really an option, so you tried and you tried hard. It was a healthy place to grow academically - be like your friends and do well. I enjoyed those days. They taught me there was always someone doing better, no matter how much you thought you knew.

School instilled within me a fear of not living up to expectation.  It was a psychological game, a useful one academically, but an unhealthy one in the long run. Without a proper long term goal steering this relentless pursuit of grades, my life became about the pat on the back from the teacher. And about winning the respect of my classmates. I became very, very fearful of failure.

University was the same thing, just scaled up. The rewards for my try-harding of high school was a place studying engineering at Imperial College. This is where my personal lack of discipline first took hold.
