*The seeds of a mindset framework, and the daily practice of growing into the person you wish to become.*

Dear readers,

There comes a point when the desire to change asks something more of us than enthusiasm. We have read the books, heard the lectures, felt that spirited conviction that a different way of living is possible. Yet the real work begins the following morning, when the feeling has faded and the familiar habits are waiting.

This post presents a few seedlings of a mindset framework. My hope is that, with a little care & consistent attention, they may take root in your own daily life.

---

### Mindset seedlings

The *ultimate* goal is to develop the ability to act in accordance with your intentions, even when worry or hesitation comes along for the journey. To steadily move towards something meaningful, there must be a set of principles in place; something you can return to when the mood of the day begins to pull you elsewhere.

Sooner or later, the student of their own mind realises that the discomfort of repeatedly neglecting what matters to them can *far* outweigh the fleeting discomfort of beginning.

So the diligent person learns to keep small promises. They sit down to the work. They make time for reflection. They begin again after a difficult day. To an outsider, this seems easy — just do what you said you would do, right? But to reach this level requires a certain [self-trust]{.glossary definition="Confidence in your ability to follow through, make considered decisions and respond when things go wrong."}, one that grows through the quiet evidence of your own actions.

It is wise to begin & end the day with a little room for gratitude. Before the demands of the world arrive, give a moment to what is already here: a person you value, an opportunity to learn, a piece of work worth doing. There need be nothing grand about it. The practice lies in noticing.

### The concept you hold of yourself

> *“There is no other way to change in this world. ‘I am the way’. My ‘I AMness’, my consciousness is the way by which I change my world. As I change my concept of myself, I change the world.” — Neville Goddard*

There is something worth considering in this idea of [self-concept]{.glossary definition="The beliefs and descriptions you hold about who you are, including what you believe you are capable of."}. Listen for a moment to the statements you habitually make about yourself. *I never follow through. I am always behind. I am simply not that sort of person.*

Repeated often enough, these descriptions can begin to feel like instructions.

What might happen if you gave yourself a more generous instruction? *I am learning to be consistent. I can approach this with care. I am someone who returns to the work.*

You need not feel a resolute confidence every morning. Give the new belief something tangible to rest upon. If you wish to become attentive, practise giving one task your undivided attention. If you wish to become dependable, keep one modest promise today. Let [self-belief]{.glossary definition="A belief in your capacity to learn, act and develop, even when a particular outcome remains uncertain."} find expression in something you can actually do.

### The Bridge of Incidents

Among the works and lectures of Neville Goddard lies the concept of the [Bridge of Incidents]{.glossary definition="Goddard’s idea that a sequence of apparently unrelated events leads towards an assumed outcome; used here as a reflective metaphor for an unfolding journey."}. These incidents may at first appear random, yet, within his teaching, they form part of the journey towards a desired outcome.

I find value in holding this as a way of reflecting on experience. A disappointment may reveal something that needs attention. An unexpected conversation may open a possibility. A period of slow progress may ask us to reconsider how we are working.

We cannot know in advance what each event will come to mean. There is room, however, to meet it with curiosity before passing a final judgement.

Whatever happens during your week, I hereby set you the **task** of pausing before you decide what it says about your future. Try the thought: *“Oh. Maybe this is a part of the journey I do not yet understand.”*

Then ask: *“What can I learn here, and what can I do next?”*

This small act of [reframing]{.glossary definition="Considering another interpretation of an experience while still acknowledging the facts and how you feel."} leaves room for a response. Some circumstances call for persistence; others call for a change of direction. Give yourself permission to notice the difference.

### A little more equanimity

At the end of each day, note down a few things that relate to the person you are trying to become. Where did you keep faith with an intention? Where did your attention wander? What would you like to approach with greater care tomorrow?

Keep the answers honest and the practice small enough to repeat.

Over time, these observations may become a useful record of progress that would otherwise go unnoticed. The first attempt. The difficult conversation. The morning you began despite feeling uncertain. There is something deeply encouraging about seeing your own effort accumulate.

As you go forth into the week, my hope is that you can meet its demands with a little more [equanimity]{.glossary definition="A steadiness of mind that allows you to experience difficulty or excitement without immediately being carried away by it."} and a little more *grace*. Allow a difficult feeling to be present without giving it the final word on what you are capable of.

Thank you for reading. I trust you were able to take something of value from this, even if it is only the intention to keep one small promise to yourself today.

You are now on a journey. A journey to cultivate a steadier mind, to give patient attention to things previously neglected, to discover through experience what you may yet become.

A journey of faith.
